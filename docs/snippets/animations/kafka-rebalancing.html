Animated walkthroughConsumer group rebalancing — cooperative sticky, eager, and a crash

1 · Scale out (cooperative sticky)

  1. Two pods in the inventory group share four partitions. Each partition has exactly one owner, which is how per-key ordering survives.
  2. A third pod starts and joins the group. The coordinator triggers a rebalance.
  3. With cooperative sticky assignment, only partitions that must move are revoked. consumer-2 gives up partition 3, and Spring Kafka commits its processed offsets in the revoke callback first.
  4. consumer-3 picks up partition 3 at the committed offset. Partitions 0–2 never stopped, and nothing was read twice.

2 · Eager rebalance

  1. The same scale-out, using the older eager protocol.
  2. Every consumer revokes all of its partitions and stops: a stop-the-world pause for the whole group, even though only one partition needed to move.
  3. Partitions are reassigned and everyone resumes. During a rolling deploy this repeats for every pod, which is why cooperative sticky is the modern default and static membership (group.instance.id) helps restarts.

3 · Crash and replay

  1. consumer-2 is mid-batch on partition 3. It has processed up to offset 120 but last committed 100, because offsets are committed after processing.
  2. Pod B crashes. Nothing more gets committed, and the coordinator only notices when heartbeats stop and session.timeout.ms expires.
  3. The coordinator removes consumer-2 from the group and rebalances its partitions.
  4. consumer-1 resumes partition 3 from the last committed offset, 100. Records 100–119 are processed a second time.
  5. That's at-least-once delivery working as designed. The defence is an idempotent consumer (§2.9): an inbox table or a conditional update makes the replay harmless.
